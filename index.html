How this works ▾

Which estimate drives your targets: the Kalman filter. It runs one step per day over your whole log, uses ~3,200 kcal/lb during a cut (a high-protein cut loses some lean tissue too), subtracts carb-driven water swings, downweights odd weigh-ins and gives a usable number from the first days of a phase. In simulated cuts its error was ~30–45% lower than the regression's at every point. The regression below is kept as a second opinion: if the two disagree beyond their bands for weeks, suspect under-logging or the scale.

Energy balance: maintenance = average intake − (weight trend × 3,500 kcal/lb). If you ate 1,750/day and lost 1.6 lb/week, you were 800 kcal/day under maintenance, so maintenance ≈ 2,550.

Why a regression: daily weight swings 1–3 lb on water, sodium, glycogen and gut contents, while real tissue change is ~0.1–0.2 lb/day. Fitting a line through every weigh-in uses all the data, so the slope firms up much faster than comparing two weekly averages.

The ± range is a 95% interval from the scatter of your weigh-ins around the line, widened for day-to-day water carryover (a salty meal or a hard leg day moves weight for 2–3 days, so neighbouring weigh-ins aren't independent). It shrinks roughly with days1.5. With typical carryover that's about ±250 at 4 steady weeks and ±150 at 6; consistent food, sodium and weigh-in timing tighten it. The band was calibrated by simulation to contain the true value ~95% of the time.

What it can't see: logging errors. If intake is under-logged by 200 kcal, maintenance reads 200 low. It also can't separate fat from water, so any intake change resets glycogen/water for ~10 days. Use the start-date filter to skip that.

Phases: when you start a cut, maintenance phase or bulk, the first 10 days are skipped automatically (glycogen and water are still shifting, which would bias the slope by ~200 kcal). Until the new phase has enough data, the estimate from the end of the previous phase carries forward.

Intake timing: a morning weigh-in reflects food through the night before, so intake is counted from the first weigh-in day up to (not including) the last one, each day weighted exactly as the regression weights that day's weight change.

Kalman estimate: a second estimator shown below the regression. Instead of fitting one line to a window, it updates a running estimate every day: it starts from a Katch–McArdle guess (± 300), weighs each weigh-in by how much it can tell, downweights readings that look like water or gut noise, subtracts the glycogen swing implied by your logged carbs, uses 3,200 kcal/lb in a cut (a high-protein cut loses ~10–15% lean, so a pound isn't a full 3,500 kcal of fat), and keeps its memory across phase changes instead of discarding 10 days. Its band is tighter early and honest late. The two should agree within their bands after ~4 steady weeks; if they keep disagreeing, under-logging is the usual cause.

Settings

Sync your log across devices through a secret gist on your GitHub account. Every save is kept in the gist’s revision history.

First-time setup (2 min) ▾
  1. On GitHub: Settings → Developer settings → Personal access tokens → Fine-grained tokens → Generate new token.
  2. Name it “HDH Log”, pick an expiration, and leave Repository access on Public repositories.
  3. Under Account permissions, set Gists → Read and write. Nothing else.
  4. Generate, copy the token, paste it below, and tap Save & Sync. Leave Gist ID blank: the app finds or creates the gist.
  5. On your other devices, paste the same token and Save & Sync. They find the same gist on their own.
Secret gists are unlisted, not private: anyone with the gist’s exact link can view it. Don’t share the link. The token can only touch your gists, and it’s stored only in this browser.
