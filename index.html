How this works ▾

Energy balance: maintenance = average intake − (weight trend × 3,500 kcal/lb). If you ate 1,750/day and lost 1.6 lb/week, you were 800 kcal/day under maintenance, so maintenance ≈ 2,550.

Why a regression: daily weight swings 1–3 lb on water, sodium, glycogen and gut contents, while real tissue change is ~0.1–0.2 lb/day. Fitting a line through every weigh-in uses all the data, so the slope firms up much faster than comparing two weekly averages.

The ± range is a 95% interval from the scatter of your weigh-ins around the line, widened for day-to-day water carryover (a salty meal or a hard leg day moves weight for 2–3 days, so neighbouring weigh-ins aren't independent). It shrinks roughly with days1.5. With typical carryover that's about ±250 at 4 steady weeks and ±150 at 6; consistent food, sodium and weigh-in timing tighten it. The band was calibrated by simulation to contain the true value ~95% of the time.

What it can't see: logging errors. If intake is under-logged by 200 kcal, maintenance reads 200 low. It also can't separate fat from water, so any intake change resets glycogen/water for ~10 days. Use the start-date filter to skip that.

Phases: when you start a cut, maintenance phase or bulk, the first 10 days are skipped automatically (glycogen and water are still shifting, which would bias the slope by ~200 kcal). Until the new phase has enough data, the estimate from the end of the previous phase carries forward.

Intake timing: a morning weigh-in reflects food through the night before, so intake is counted from the first weigh-in day up to (not including) the last one, each day weighted exactly as the regression weights that day's weight change.
